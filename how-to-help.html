Every dollar goes to the cats

Roadcat Rescue is a lean, volunteer-powered operation — no office lease, no bureaucracy. The overwhelming majority of every donation goes directly to vet care, trapping supplies, and colony management — the field work itself.

A single spay/neuter surgery costs approximately $75 at our vet partners. A complete TNR kit — traps, transport crates, and supplies — runs around $300 and serves a colony for years. Your $25 matters. Your $100 matters more.

  • $25 — Covers vaccines for one cat
  • $75 — Covers a spay/neuter surgery
  • $200 — Fully TNRs two cats, start to finish
  • $500 — Launches a new colony program

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Why not just remove the cats?

Removal doesn't work — it's been tried for decades. When cats are removed, the food source and shelter remain, and new unaltered cats move in and breed. Biologists call it the “vacuum effect.” TNR is the only approach shown to stabilize and gradually shrink colonies while ending the suffering of endless kitten litters.

Is TNR actually proven?

Yes. Trap-Neuter-Return is endorsed by the ASPCA, the Humane Society of the United States, and Alley Cat Allies, and is practiced by thousands of municipalities and rescue groups nationwide. Long-term studies show colony populations decline significantly over time when sustained TNR is in place.

Is my donation tax-deductible?

Our 501(c)(3) application is in progress. Once the IRS approves it, tax-deductible status is typically retroactive to our incorporation date — and we will notify every early donor so you can claim your deduction. Until then, please give knowing your support is real even if the paperwork is still catching up.

Why rest stops specifically?

Rest stop colonies fall through every crack: urban TNR groups don't travel to them, shelters can't take feral cats, and state-managed land requires coordination most nonprofits never pursue. Travelers feed the cats (keeping colonies anchored), but no one fixes them. That's the gap Roadcat Rescue exists to fill.

What happens to kittens and friendly cats?

Truly feral adult cats are returned to their colony — it's the only home they know, and they are not adoptable. But young kittens and socialized strays we encounter are pulled from the colony and placed with foster and adoption partners whenever possible.
